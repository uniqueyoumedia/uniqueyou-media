What we actually did

She films her own days on her phone. Setting up the Sunday brunch, asking her team what their favourite dish is, carrying a bowl of beans through the kitchen in her apron. Nothing staged, nothing lit.

We shape it, write it in her voice, plan it and publish it. Every week we check in, because on your own this is exactly the thing that sinks into the everyday chaos of running a restaurant.

The reels that go furthest are never the polished ones. They are the ones where she is simply herself in her own kitchen. The reach came from who she already was.

Same owner, second business

From product shots to people.

Mary also runs a farm and gift shop. Its Instagram looked the way most small shops look: nicely arranged product photos, posted whenever there was time, and not a single person in the picture.

Now her face is in the feed. Her team, her customers, the corner of the shop she rebuilt. Published on a rhythm instead of whenever there was a gap.

Nothing about the products changed. What changed is that you can tell who is behind them.
